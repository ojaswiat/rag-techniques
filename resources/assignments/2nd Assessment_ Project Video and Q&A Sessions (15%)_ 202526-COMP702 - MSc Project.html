2nd Assessment: Project Video and Q&A Sessions (15%)

This assessment consists of two parts. First, a 10 minute video of your software or your project (including a short introduction). Second, a 10-15 minute discussion with both markers. Therefore you need to prepare a few slides, plan and record your video, and be prepared to open files and show your screen so you can answer questions during the discussion. Take note of any verbal feedback, as we often give advice that you can incorporate into the dissertation.

NOTE: You must upload a video by the deadline, and you must attend the Q&A session which is usually arranged the week after the video deadline. If either part is missing, you'll score zero (absent) for this assessment. The discussion can't be rescheduled if you miss it. If you have a genuine reason why you can't attend (supported by a valid extenuating circumstances claim), then your markers will base their mark on what you put in the video.

If your video is longer than 10 minutes, there will be penalty of 10 points for every full minute. For example, if your video is 10 minutes 59 seconds, there is not penalty, but if your video is 11 minutes 0 seconds, you will get penalty of 10 points. Similarly, if you video is 12 minutes 0 second, you will get a penalty of 20 points.

If your video is longer than 15 minutes, we will not watch your video and your CA2 will be marked 0 immediately. 

How to submit?

You should submit your media recording in the CA2 section in the assignment section: CA2 - Video Presentation and Q&A (15%)

Be aware of the marking guidelines for this assessment.

Feedback will be available via the e-Project SystemLinks to an external site. when the work has been marked.

Check the timetable for the deadline.

The structure of the presentation

Your presentation should have the following structure.

Statement of Ethical Compliance

On the title page of your video slides you should state the data and participant categories for your project (for example, A0, C2, etc.) with a sentence to confirm that you followed our ethical guidance. You can go into more detail about participant activities and data sources if they form a central part of the project, but the focus of the video should be on demonstrating your software and development tools. You will be asked about project ethics during the discussion.

Video Introduction

Begin by giving a general overview of your project, the motivation behind it, the things you hoped to achieve, and any major conclusions. Show how your original objectives and design influenced the final software product. This should only last a couple of minutes so you won't need lots of slides. If the project didn't go to plan, you should explain what happened. Remember that a lot of this will also be included in your dissertation, so pick out the highlights and things you really want to emphasise to the markers. Keep it short so you have more time to demo the software.

Video Screen Recording

Spend the rest of the time showing your software in use. Try to cover a variety of possible interactions with the software, including different types of user if applicable (such as admin and ordinary users). You should also show your development environment, including source code. If there is a particular piece of code that you're proud of, this is the time to talk about it. However, please bear in mind that the entire video should be no longer than 10 minutes. Use the time wisely to convey what your project is about and how it all works. We do not need to see explanations for every source file, since this just wastes time. Put yourself in the position of a user and demonstrate what the software does.

Recording and submitting your video

Remember that your video should include a short introduction, but if you spend too much time on this you won't have enough time to demonstrate your software. You can make separate recordings and edit them together, or you can just move from your slides to your software within the same recording.

Your video must be narrated in your own voice. Your presentation skills and English language proficiency are part of the assessment, so please don't use text-to-speech software to generate your narration.

 Mac
If you are using a Mac, the easiest way to record your screen is to use Quicktime Player, which should already be installed. From its File menu select New Screen Recording and follow the prompts. You can use iMovie to edit your video and arrange multiple scenes.

 Windows /  Linux /  Mac
If you are using Windows or Linux, the best approach is to download OBS (Open Broadcaster Software). This is also available for Mac if you don't want to use Quicktime. To edit your video and combine multiple scenes, use the Photos app in Windows. There are many video editing packages available for Linux.

Q&A session

Your supervisor will liaise with you to find out when you're free in the week following the deadline. The discussion will involve both markers (supervisor and second marker), so you might have to miss a lab or tutorial session to make scheduling easier. It will probably take place in person, in a staff office or meeting room, but it can happen over Teams or Zoom if everyone agrees in advance. Expect an informal chat, not an inquisition. There is no need to dress up for the meeting.

The discussion will last for around 10 minutes. It's a chance for the markers to clarify any points they are unsure about after watching the video. It's also your opportunity to promote your project and get valuable advice for the dissertation. Make sure you cover everything you want to discuss, since this is the only opportunity to speak to both markers at the same time. The markers might ask you to show and talk about parts of your code, so be ready to open your development environment during the discussion.
